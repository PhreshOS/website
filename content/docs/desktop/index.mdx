title: How it works
description: What the Desktop is, what it shows, and what belongs to each one.
---

The Desktop is where a person uses the System, in a browser. It represents
the System: it shows the Programs and Windows the System keeps, and keeps
none of them itself. Close the browser and nothing stops; open the Desktop
again, or in another browser, and everything is there.

## What it shows

Every running Client, in one of five layers, from the wallpaper at the back to
the Shell, with its Taskbar and Start Menu, at the front. Standard Windows live
in the middle layer, on a plane larger than the Desktop, and the Desktop shows
one view of it at a time.

## What belongs to each Desktop

The same System can be open in several browsers at once. Each Desktop has its
own:

- **view**: the part of the plane it shows;
- **preferences**: light or dark, animations, and scale;
- **drawing**: how large each Client is drawn on its screen, in pixels.

Everything else, such as a Window's title, position, and size, is kept by the
System and shared by every Desktop.

## From the terminal

The terminal is not a Desktop: it has no view and no preferences. It reaches
what the System keeps, such as Windows and the browsers connected:

```sh
phresh window inspect --process main --program notes
phresh connection list
```
