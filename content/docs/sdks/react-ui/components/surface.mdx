A `Surface` is a painted area: a color, a material, an edge, corners, and a
shadow. Every React UI component that draws something is built from it. Use it
directly for your own regions, such as a card or a sidebar.

```tsx
<Surface style={{ padding: 20 }}>
  <h2>Storage</h2>
  <p>4.2 GB available</p>
</Surface>
```

A Surface paints; it does not decide padding, layout, or overflow. Set those
with `style` or `className`, or use a component that has a structure, such as
[`Panel`](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/panel) or
[`Window`](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/window).

## Depth

`depth` says where a Surface sits relative to what is around it:

| `depth` | Means | Looks | Used by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `raised` (default) | Something you act on | A lit edge and a soft shadow | Buttons, windows, menus, the selected tab |
| `flat` | Level with its surroundings | A softer edge | Regions and groups |
| `recessed` | Something that holds a value you enter | Slightly sunk, a dim edge | Text fields, tracks, switches when off |
| `none` | Part of its surroundings | Nothing at rest; hover and press show as a veil | Quiet actions, the items of lists and menus |

```tsx
<Surface>Raised</Surface>
<Surface depth="flat">Flat</Surface>
<Surface depth="recessed">Holds content</Surface>
<Surface depth="none">Part of what is around it</Surface>
```

Without depth, a Surface paints nothing and has no edge or shadow until it is
hovered, pressed, or focused. A meaning color, such as `danger`, then colors its
content instead of a fill.

Every other depth uses the same edge: light along the top and bottom, faint along
the sides, and a fine dark line around it. How deep a recess sinks and how
bright the edge is follow the background's actual lightness, so the effect
matches on any background.

A raised or flat Surface inside another keeps apart from it: when its color
would be too close to the one beneath, it steps lighter, or darker when there
is no room, until the difference shows. So a card on a card, or a button on a
dialog of the same color, still stands out, however deep the nesting goes. A
recessed Surface keeps its own constant depth instead, so a field reads the
same wherever it is, unless it would vanish into what it sits on: a field or a
checkbox in a well of the same color sinks one step below the well.

A layer that floats above everything, a popover, a menu, a dialog, a tooltip,
or a panel, is not inside the Surface it was opened from, so it keeps its own
color there.

## Material

`material` sets how much of the Appearance material is drawn, each named by
what it draws:

| `material` | Draws |
| --- | --- |
| `none` | The color only |
| `opaque` | The color, grain, and the edge; solid whatever the Appearance opacity |
| `translucent` (default) | Also the material's opacity |
| `frosted` | Also the blur, saturation, and refraction of what lies behind it |

```tsx
<Surface material="frosted">Frosted</Surface>
<Surface material={{ opacity: 0.7, backdropBlur: 18 }}>Custom frosted</Surface>
<Surface material={{ mode: "translucent", opacity: 0.35 }}>Fainter, without frost</Surface>
```

An object applies your overrides to `frosted`, or to the mode its `mode`
names. Over something already frosted, such as a button on a frosted bar, use
`translucent`: frosting it again only blurs the same backdrop twice. Backdrop effects are skipped
whenever the result is opaque, since nothing behind would show through.

`grain` moves the grains' count and strength against each other: as it rises
there are fewer grains, each stronger. At `0` every pixel holds a grain no one
sees, and at `1` none is left, so neither end draws anything.

## Color, radius, and shadow

```tsx
<Surface color="primary:subtle">Highlighted</Surface>
<Surface radius="full">Pill</Surface>
<Surface shadow={false}>No shadow</Surface>
<Surface shadow="large">A stronger shadow</Surface>
```

The default color is `background`. Text inside a colored Surface takes the
most readable color automatically. See [Color](/docs/sdks/react-ui/color) and
[Size and radius](/docs/sdks/react-ui/scale).

Only a `raised` Surface casts a shadow. It falls evenly on every side at one
fixed blur, so `shadow` sets only its strength: a level relative to the
Appearance, or a number.

## Choose the element

`as` renders another element or component, without an extra wrapper:

```tsx
<Surface as="section" aria-labelledby="summary">
  <h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>
</Surface>
```

A custom component must put `className`, `style`, and `children` on one element
and forward its ref.

Your `className` and `style` always win over the Surface's own styles.

## Props

| Prop | Default | Sets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `depth` | `"raised"` | `raised`, `flat`, `recessed`, or `none` |
| `color` | `"background"` | Its color |
| `material` | `"translucent"` | `none`, `opaque`, `translucent`, `frosted`, or material overrides |
| `radius` | The Appearance radius | Its corners |
| `shadow` | The Appearance shadow | `false`, `true`, a level, or a strength; raised only |
| `as` | `"div"` | The element to render |
