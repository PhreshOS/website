`AppLayout` frames a program: a title above a sidebar, and a header, the
content, and an optional footer beside them. It owns the regions, their
scrolling, and the content Surface. The program places its own components inside:

```tsx
<AppLayout>
  <AppLayout.Title>Settings</AppLayout.Title>
  <AppLayout.Sidebar aria-label="Settings">
    <nav aria-label="Settings"><Tree aria-label="Sections" selectionMode="single">…</Tree></nav>
  </AppLayout.Sidebar>
  <AppLayout.Header><Palette />Appearance</AppLayout.Header>
  <AppLayout.Content>…</AppLayout.Content>
  <AppLayout.Footer><Button>Discard</Button><Button color="primary">Save</Button></AppLayout.Footer>
</AppLayout>
```

Put it inside [`Window.Content`](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/window), or fill a
Program's Client page with it.

## Regions

Only the content is a Surface: the same inset region as
[Panel content](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/panel), with the `extended`
material and the Appearance radius. It is recessed: pressed into what holds the
layout, such as a Window, rather than raised on it. `depth="raised"` lifts it
instead. The title, the sidebar, the
header, and the footer have no Surface. They sit on whatever holds the layout,
such as a Window, and spacing alone separates them.

- The title and the header share one row, as tall as a Window or Panel header.
- The sidebar and the content each scroll on their own. What is given as the
  sidebar's `footer` stays at its foot while the rest of it scrolls, such as
  work in progress.
- The content is padded as a container, the same as Panel content, and
  clips what scrolls inside it to its corners.
- The layout sits `small` spacing inside whatever holds it.
- The sidebar is eighteen times the `spacing` wide unless `sidebarWidth` says
  otherwise.

While there is no content to show, `AppLayout.Placeholder` stands in for it,
in the middle of what shows of the content: a wait, what went wrong, or that
there is nothing:

```tsx
<AppLayout.Content>
  {loading ? <AppLayout.Placeholder><Spinner label="Reading" /></AppLayout.Placeholder> : <Files />}
</AppLayout.Content>
```

A [`Tree`](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/tree) in the sidebar makes a navigation
that branches: sections that open into pages.

## A narrow window

At 640 pixels or less, the sidebar would crowd the content out. The layout then
gives the content its whole width, and the title and sidebar wait in a
[`Drawer`](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/drawer). `AppLayout.SidebarToggle`
opens it; put it in the header, where it shows only while the layout is narrow:

```tsx
<AppLayout.Header><AppLayout.SidebarToggle />Appearance</AppLayout.Header>
```

The width is the layout's own, not the window's. Choosing something in the
sidebar is the program's, so it puts the Drawer away:

```tsx
const { narrow, closeSidebar } = useAppLayout()
```

## Parts

| Part | Is |
| --- | --- |
| `AppLayout` | The frame. `sidebarWidth` sets the sidebar's width |
| `AppLayout.Title` | The program's title above the sidebar, an `h1` |
| `AppLayout.Sidebar` | The sidebar, an `aside`. `footer` stays at its foot |
| `AppLayout.Header` | The row above the content, a `header` |
| `AppLayout.Content` | The content on its Surface, the page's `main`. `depth` is `recessed` (default) or `raised` |
| `AppLayout.Placeholder` | What stands in the middle of the content while there is none to show |
| `AppLayout.Footer` | An optional row below the content, a `footer` |
| `AppLayout.SidebarToggle` | Opens the sidebar while the layout is narrow; otherwise nothing |
