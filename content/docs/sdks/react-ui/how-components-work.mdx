title: How components work
description: Every component is a Surface; what it is decides which props it takes. Learn the kinds once and the rest follows.
---

Every component that draws something is a [Surface](/docs/sdks/react-ui/components/surface):
a painted area with a color, a depth, a material, and corners. What a
component *is* decides which of those you set, so components of the same kind
take the same props. Every component also takes `className` and `style`, and
yours always win over React UI's.

## Four kinds

**Controls** are what you act on or fill in: Button, Input, Select, Checkbox,
Switch, Slider, and the date, time, number, and color fields. You set their
`color`, `size`, `radius`, `material`, and `disabled`. They have a size
because they sit in a line of text and other controls and must match it.

```tsx
<Button color="primary" size="large">Install</Button>
<Input size="small" label="Filter" disabled />
```

**Containers** hold your content: Surface, Panel, Window, Dialog, Popover,
Tooltip, Alert. You set the Surface itself: `color`, `depth`, `material`,
`shadow`, `radius`. They have no `size`, because their size is their content's.

```tsx
<Panel material="extended">…</Panel>
<Surface depth="flat" color="info:soft">…</Surface>
```

**Collections** are lists of items: ListBox, Menu, GridList, Tabs, Table,
Tree, Accordion. You set `size`, `radius`, and `disabled` for all their items,
and `color` for the part you choose, such as the selected item.

**Marks** label something: Badge, Avatar, tags. You set `color` and `size`.

A few components have a single look with nothing to set, such as Snippet and
App Layout.

## Depth follows what a thing is

A Surface is `raised` when it stands on its own or you act on it, and
`recessed` when it holds something. Nesting alternates them, which is what
gives an interface its depth:

- a raised container, such as a Window or a card;
- inside it, a recessed area that holds content, such as a field or a list;
- on that area, raised or flat items you choose from.

A recessed thing inside a recessed thing loses the effect, so keep the
alternation.

## Fields

Everything that holds a value is a field and is written the same way:

```tsx
<Input
  label="Email"
  description="Used only for sign-in."
  invalid={!valid}
  errorMessage="Enter a valid email."
  value={email}
  onChange={setEmail}
/>
```

Pass `value` and `onChange` to own the value, or `defaultValue`, or nothing, to
let the field keep it. Checkbox and Switch use `checked` and `defaultChecked`,
since their value is on or off. Without a visible `label`, name the field with
`aria-label`.

## Items

Collections are written as the family, then its items, each with a string `id`:

```tsx
<Select label="Program" value={program} onChange={setProgram}>
  <Select.Item id="notes">Notes</Select.Item>
  <Select.Item id="tilo" disabled>Tilo</Select.Item>
</Select>
```

The selection is the `id` of the selected item, or a list of them when several
can be selected. Actions report the `id` through `onAction(id)`. RadioGroup is
the one exception: its items use `value`, because that is what a form submits.

## Parts

A component with parts exposes them on itself, and the parts work only inside
it:

```tsx
<Dialog>
  <Dialog.Trigger>Rename</Dialog.Trigger>
  <Dialog.Backdrop>
    <Dialog.Content>
      <Dialog.Title>Rename note</Dialog.Title>
      …
    </Dialog.Content>
  </Dialog.Backdrop>
</Dialog>
```

Overlays share one shape: the root owns whether it is open, `Trigger` opens
it, and `Content` is the floating Surface around your content. Focus, keyboard
handling, closing, and entrance and exit belong to the component.

## Pressing

Pressable parts use `onPress`, which behaves the same for mouse, touch, and
keyboard:

```tsx
<Button onPress={save}>Save</Button>
```

`pending` keeps a Button focusable but ignores presses while something runs;
`disabled` removes it from focus entirely.
